The entry was **accurate for the four axes it names** and silent about a fifth surface the module has since
grown. Read literally, its own Violation signal — *"a raw status/type accepted instead of a rule name"* —
classified `salesRepCustomerOrders`' **correct** behaviour as a violation. That is the phantom-failure
class: a stale oracle manufactures FAILs rather than erroring, and 17 cases plus all future authoring on
that surface read this entry as their oracle.

**The remedy is "scope the invariant", not "change the query", and that was not a judgement call.** The
module README states the divergence as design intent in its own words — *"Unlike the rule-based lists
above, these two follow **X-Order's** shape: `filter` is a search phrase, `facet` names index fields, and
`sort` takes index field expressions. They read the order **index**, not the database"* — and PR #14
devotes a section to why the new surface deliberately does not adopt the rule vocabulary.
