# BL Audit — 2026-09-19 · dangling-citation scope

**Scope as invoked:** `BL-SEC-004`, `BL-PAY-005`, and `CAT-040`/`CAT-041` citing configurable-product
rules on SEO cases. **Axis:** `bl`. **Deterministic core:** `npm run bl:lint`.

**Headline: the four items named are instances of a systemic condition, not isolated typos.**
`bl:lint` reports **~85 dangling citations across four invariant families that do not exist at all**.
All are held in `BLC_002_BASELINE` as known debt, which is why the gate was green.

| Family | In oracle? | Citing cases | Note |
|---|---|--:|---|
| `BL-GA4-001..004` | **No such family** | ~33 | analytics |
| `BL-SEC-001..005` | **No such family** | ~26 | security suite 044 + PCI cases |
| `BL-CFG-003/004/007/008` | **No such family** | ~21 | configurable products |
| `BL-PAY-002`, `BL-PAY-006` | Family exists; these IDs never did | 5 | `BL-PAY` holds only 001/003/004 |

For comparison, the oracle's largest real families are `BL-SR` (32) and `BL-LOY` (19) — so the
**missing** `BL-SEC` family would be mid-sized. This is not a rounding error in the corpus.

## 1. Applied — ratchet shrinks, no oracle body change

Per BLC-002's *fix + de-baseline* rule (and never the reverse — a baseline number is never widened
to make a build green):

| Change | Before | After | Why |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| `BL-PAY-005` baseline entry | 6 | **deleted** | All 6 citations purged from suite 041 in the `REG-2026-09-19-1035` triage. `bl:lint` had already flagged the entry as *stale — no longer dangling* |
| `BL-SEC-004` baseline | 6 | **5** | One citation cleared on `SEC-AUTH-002`, whose row was rebuilt in the same pass |

`bl:lint` exits **0**; the stale-baseline warning is gone.

**Why purging `BL-PAY-005` cost no traceability:** every one of its 6 citations *co-cited* the real
`BL-PAY-003`. Removing the phantom left each case still grounded.

## 2. Applied — the two REMAPs (`CAT-040`, `CAT-041`)

Both cited `BL-CAT-006` (*Configurable product requires all sections filled*) and `BL-CAT-001`
(*Stock zero disables purchase*) — **configurable-product and stock rules on SEO cases**. Wrong domain.

Repointed to **`BL-SEO-003`**, which both cases already cited **inline in their own assertion text**
(*"per BL-SEO-003: this theme uses og:url as its sole canonical-URL signal — no `link[rel=canonical]`
is emitted, by design"*). The grounding came from the cases themselves, not from a guess — this is a
**REMAP**, the safe half of the dangling-citation rule.

## 3. NOT applied — `BL-SEC-*` promotion, and the reason matters

A dangling citation resolves either by **REMAP** (point at the right existing ID) or **ADD** (promote
a genuinely missing invariant). For `BL-SEC-*` there is nothing to remap to — the family is absent —
so ADD is the only route. **I am not proposing it yet, and the blocker is upstream.**

**Suite 044, which carries most `BL-SEC-*` citations, is ~85% non-executable.** 29 of its 34 rows have
**both `Steps` and `Assertions` empty** (classifier code `EX-002`, independently confirmed three times
across two runs; `suites:review` reports 180 findings, 142 blocking).

**An invariant promoted to ground a case that does not say what it tests is an invented requirement.**
The citation tells us a case *wanted* a security invariant; it cannot tell us *which behaviour*, because
the row is empty. Promoting `BL-SEC-004` to satisfy `SEC-AUTH-001`/`-003`/`SEC-SESSION-*` would be
writing the oracle from a title.

**Correct order:** author suite 044's empty rows (`/qa-test-lifecycle`, not an auto-fix) → see what the
cases actually assert → *then* triangulate those behaviours on the 3-source bar and promote what earns it.

A second reason for caution: several `BL-SEC-*` citations sit on generic OWASP hygiene (XSS, SQLi, path
traversal, CORS, CSRF). This oracle carries **business-logic invariants** — VC-specific rules whose
violation has a named product cost. Generic web-security hygiene may belong in a checklist rather than
here. That is a scoping decision for the oracle owner, not something to settle by promoting five IDs.

## 4. Candidate gap worth recording — the decline path

`BL-PAY-003` is *"Successful card payment creates a paid order with a recorded transaction"* — the
**success** path only. Nothing in the oracle states the converse: **a DECLINED payment must not create
an order.** That is what suite 041's decline family was reaching for when it cited the phantom
`BL-PAY-005`.

This is a genuine candidate, but it is **not promotable from this audit**: it needs the 3-source bar,
and the decline path on this env is currently **unverifiable** — the CyberSource decline fixture is a
Stripe PAN copied across four processors, so `PAY-DECLINE-001` plus 6 cascaded cases have never once
exercised a decline (see the run's triage report §1). **Verify the decline trigger first; the invariant
follows the evidence, not the other way round.**

## 5. The write tool — `npm run bl:remap`

`scripts/knowledge/remap-bl-citations.ts` is the mechanical arm for this whole problem, and it is the
right instrument for every citation change recorded above. Its three modes map exactly onto the
decisions an audit makes:

| Mode | Use when | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `--from X --to Y` | An existing invariant already covers it (**REMAP**) | **Refuses if Y is absent** — "the one thing worse than a dangling citation is a remap that creates another" |
| `--propose X` | X is a legitimate ADD candidate awaiting triangulation | Rewrites to `PROPOSED-BL-X`, which `lint-bl.ts` **exempts from BLC-002 by design** — converts false traceability into a *declared* forward-reference without fabricating anything |
| `--drop X --reason "…"` | The ref is simply wrong | Removes it; reason is mandatory and goes in the report, never into the CSV |

Dry by default; `--apply` writes; touches `Business_Rule` **and nothing else**, verified cell-by-cell;
and **preserves each file's own line endings**.

> **Recorded against myself:** the `BL-PAY-005` purge in §1 was done by hand instead of with
> `bl:remap --drop`, and the same hand-rolled CSV surgery wrote bare LFs into a CRLF file, took
> `suites:lint` down with `CSV_INVALID_CLOSING_QUOTE`, and had to be repaired. The tool exists
> precisely to prevent that. **Use `bl:remap` for citation edits; hand-edit only what it cannot express**
> — e.g. the `CAT-040`/`CAT-041` remaps, which had to be per-case because a global
> `BL-CAT-006 → BL-SEO-003` would have hit **216** citing cases instead of 2.

## 6. Recommendation — and the rule that decides `--propose` vs author-first

**A forward-reference is only honest on a row that says what it tests.** Measured today:

| Suite | Family | Authored rows | Empty rows |
|---|---|--:|--:|
| 043 google-analytics | `BL-GA4-*` | **33** | 0 |
| 086 reviews-graphql | `BL-CR-*` | **16** | 0 |
| 044 security | `BL-SEC-*` | 6 | **28** |

1. **`--propose` the authored families** (`BL-GA4-*`, `BL-CR-*`, `BL-CMS-*`, `BL-CFG-*`). Their citing
   cases assert real behaviour, so `PROPOSED-BL-X` states something true: *this case intends to verify a
   rule not yet promoted.* That removes false traceability without inventing an invariant.
2. **Do NOT `--propose` `BL-SEC-*`.** All five `BL-SEC-004` citations sit on rows with **both `Steps` and
   `Assertions` empty** (verified). Relabelling them would shrink the ratchet while improving nothing —
   and the ratchet is currently the *only* record that those cases claim traceability they do not have.
   **Author suite 044 first** (`/qa-test-lifecycle`); the citation follows the case, not the reverse.
3. **Do not promote any family to clear the ratchet.** Shrinking it by inventing invariants converts a
   visible debt into a false one.
4. **Audit `BL-GA4-*` first** of the ADD candidates — 33 citing cases, all authored, and analytics
   behaviour is concrete and source-anchored, so it is the likeliest to survive the 3-source bar.
5. Keep de-baselining as citations are legitimately fixed. Two entries shrank today; the mechanism works.
